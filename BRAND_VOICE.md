# Personal Brand — Content Strategy & Brand Voice Brief

This is the brand brief for all content in this repo (`reels/` and `Dice/`). Read it before writing or editing any script, caption, or post. Career claims still come only from `CAREER_FACTS.md`.

## 1. Brand Positioning

**Core Positioning:** Building Better Digital Products.

I create content about how digital products are actually built — from understanding business problems and making product decisions to software engineering, system design, AI, and automation.

The goal is to connect the business side with the technical side, based on real experience, practical knowledge, and honest lessons.

I don't want to position myself as a guru or someone who knows everything. I want to be seen as a practical product builder who shares what he has learned, what he is exploring, and how things work in the real world.

## 2. Main Goals

- Build a credible professional personal brand.
- Create networking and career opportunities.
- Connect with founders, CEOs, product people, and developers.
- Make complex technical and business concepts easier to understand.
- Build a long-term audience around product building, technology, and real experiences.

## 3. Target Audience

The content should serve three main audience segments:

| Audience | Target Share | What They Need |
|---|---:|---|
| Product Builders | 50% | Product management, engineering, system design, AI, and execution |
| CEOs & Co-founders | 30% | Business thinking, product decisions, MVPs, automation, and technology |
| Students & Beginners | 20% | Simplified explanations, career awareness, and foundational knowledge |

Important:
- These percentages represent the overall content strategy, not every individual video.
- Every piece of content should have ONE primary audience.
- Students can help expand reach, but views alone are not the main objective.
- Professional credibility, meaningful engagement, and career opportunities matter more than vanity metrics.

## 4. Content Pillars

1. **Product Management & Business**
   - Product strategy, roadmaps, MVPs, prioritization, Agile, product discovery, and business decisions.

2. **Software Engineering & System Design**
   - SDLC, architecture, APIs, databases, backend/frontend concepts, scalability, and technical decisions.

3. **AI, RAG & Automation**
   - LLMs, RAG, AI agents, automation workflows, practical AI applications, and technical experiments.

4. **Real Experiences & Lessons**
   - Lessons from working with startups, building products, managing teams, making mistakes, and navigating professional challenges.
   - Respect NDAs and never expose confidential company information.

5. **Simplified Technology & Career Education**
   - Explain technical and professional concepts in an accessible way.
   - Help beginners understand how the industry works without turning the entire brand into generic career advice.

## 5. Content Sources

Content should come from four sources:

- **Real Experience:** Lessons and stories from actual projects and professional work.
- **Fundamentals:** Timeless concepts that people need to understand.
- **Practical Deep Dives:** How things work, implementation examples, and technical breakdowns.
- **Industry & Emerging Topics:** New technologies, trends, tools, and changes worth discussing.

Do not rely entirely on personal experiences. Educational and practical content should remain possible even when a particular career experience cannot be publicly discussed.

## 6. Writing Style & Brand Voice

**Language:**
- Egyptian Arabic (عامية مصرية بسيطة وطبيعية).
- Keep common English technical terms in English when they sound more natural.
- Avoid forced translations of technical terminology.
- Write the way an experienced Egyptian professional would naturally speak.

**Tone:**
- Conversational, confident, practical, and approachable.
- Storytelling-driven, with light humor when appropriate.
- Honest and thoughtful, not overly formal.
- Professional without sounding corporate.
- Explain concepts without talking down to the audience.

**Very Important — Avoid:**
- Generic AI-sounding phrases.
- Overly polished or artificial Arabic.
- Motivational clichés and exaggerated claims.
- Unnecessary introductions and long definitions.
- Pretending to be an expert in topics without sufficient experience.
- Repeating the same point in different words.
- Excessive English when a simple Arabic explanation works better.

The content should sound like ME, not like a textbook, marketing agency, or AI-generated script.

## 7. Storytelling Approach

Whenever possible, use this structure:

1. **Hook:** Start with a surprising statement, relatable problem, question, or interesting situation.
2. **Context:** Introduce a real-life scenario or story that makes the topic relevant.
3. **Explanation:** Explain the concept in simple, logical steps.
4. **Example:** Use a practical example, analogy, or realistic scenario.
5. **Takeaway:** Give the audience one clear insight they can remember.
6. **Ending:** Finish naturally with a thought-provoking question or a relevant CTA when appropriate.

This is a flexible framework, NOT a mandatory template for every video.

Prefer:
- "خليني أحكيلك موقف..."
- "تخيل إنك..."
- "المشكلة هنا مش في..."
- "تعالى نفهم الموضوع بمثال..."
- "وده يخلينا نسأل سؤال مهم..."

Use these as examples of natural delivery, not repeated catchphrases.

## 8. Video Formats & Publishing Strategy

**Primary format:** Hybrid content strategy.

- One long-form YouTube video per week.
- Extract 2–3 valuable Reels/Shorts from each long video.
- Publish short-form content throughout the week.
- Repurpose valuable insights into LinkedIn posts when relevant.

**Main platforms:**
- YouTube — long-form content and deeper explanations.
- Instagram — Reels and audience engagement.
- TikTok — short-form discovery and reach.
- LinkedIn — professional credibility, networking, and career opportunities.

The short videos should work independently. Don't simply cut random sections from the long video; each Reel needs its own hook, context, and clear takeaway.

## 9. Script-Writing Rules

When writing a script:

- Start with the actual idea, not a generic greeting.
- Make the first few seconds interesting.
- Write for spoken delivery, not for reading.
- Keep sentences relatively short and natural.
- Use transitions that sound conversational.
- Explain one main idea per video.
- Use examples before complicated definitions when possible.
- Avoid unnecessary technical depth unless the target audience needs it.
- Don't force jokes.
- Don't add a CTA to every piece of content.
- Keep the script within the requested duration.

For short videos, aim for approximately 30–60 seconds unless the topic genuinely needs more time.

For long-form videos, prioritize clarity, storytelling, and useful depth over an arbitrary duration.

## 10. Content Planning Principles

- Build an evergreen content library, not just a list of weekly posts.
- Maintain a balance between beginner, intermediate, and advanced topics.
- Avoid repeating the same idea with different titles.
- Connect topics into useful series when appropriate.
- Mix educational videos with real experiences and practical demonstrations.
- Keep the next two weeks production-ready while maintaining a flexible long-term roadmap.
- Update topics when technology or industry practices change.
- Never invent personal experiences, achievements, metrics, or project outcomes.

## 11. Accuracy, Credibility & Confidentiality

This is a professional personal brand, so credibility is essential.

- Distinguish between personal experience, researched information, and personal opinion.
- Verify facts, especially when discussing AI tools, technologies, industry news, and religious or sensitive subjects.
- Never invent project details or claim that an unfinished project was launched.
- Respect NDAs and company confidentiality.
- When information is missing, ask instead of assuming.
- Be transparent when discussing experimental or evolving technologies.

## 12. Success Metrics

Evaluate the strategy through:

- Consistency of publishing.
- Reach and audience growth.
- Watch time and audience retention.
- Meaningful comments, saves, and shares.
- Audience quality and relevance.
- Networking conversations and professional opportunities.
- Improvement in storytelling and content quality.

The first three months should be treated as an experimentation and learning phase, not as a guaranteed growth or income period.

---

## Final Instruction to Claude

Before writing any content, understand the intended audience, objective, content pillar, and format.

Your job is not just to generate scripts. Your job is to help build a consistent, credible, recognizable personal brand.

Always prioritize:
**Authenticity → Clarity → Practical Value → Storytelling → Engagement.**

If a script sounds like generic AI content, rewrite it until it sounds like a real Egyptian product builder explaining something naturally to another person.
