# Research and Fact-Checking Gate

An episode cannot move to **Research Verified** until every applicable item is checked.

## Scientific research

- [ ] Prefer peer-reviewed research, systematic reviews, meta-analyses, established textbooks, and original research where necessary.
- [ ] Record complete citation details and verify title, authors, journal/publisher, year, and link/DOI.
- [ ] Separate established findings, simplified explanations, hypotheses, and analogies.
- [ ] Do not turn correlation into causation.
- [ ] State relevant limitations and distinguish laboratory findings from consumer behavior.
- [ ] Do not call dopamine simply the “happiness chemical.”
- [ ] Do not equate every form of gamification with gambling.
- [ ] Do not claim every random reward causes addiction.
- [ ] Do not claim ordinary gaming causes brain damage.
- [ ] Check relevant primary work or authoritative summaries for Skinner, Sutton and Barto, Wolfram Schultz, Deci and Ryan, and BJ Fogg when used.

## Religious research

- [ ] Use primary Hadith sources and verify collection, numbering system, Arabic text, translation, and grading.
- [ ] Separate the Hadith text from scholarly interpretation and legal application.
- [ ] Attribute each ruling to the scholar, school, or recognized institution that holds it.
- [ ] Identify disagreement where it exists; do not present a disputed issue as consensus.
- [ ] Distinguish dice, games of chance, wagering, and gambling rather than treating them as interchangeable.
- [ ] Use reputable jurisprudence references and recognized explanations.
- [ ] Do not use neuroscience to establish, invalidate, or “prove” a religious ruling.

## Editorial review

- [ ] Every factual claim has a source or is clearly framed as an example/question.
- [ ] The 45–75 second script preserves necessary nuance.
- [ ] The hook does not overpromise or misrepresent the conclusion.
- [ ] Real-world products are described fairly and without unsupported claims about intent.
- [ ] Ethical criticism distinguishes observed mechanics from inferred motives.
- [ ] Egyptian Arabic wording sounds natural when read aloud.
- [ ] English terminology is explained through context, not unnecessary jargon.
